# Common Mistakes in Physics Answers

Errors that frequently appear in explanations and solutions, including AI-generated ones.

| # | Mistake | Example | How to catch it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit mismatch | Mixing km/h and m/s in the same equation | Convert everything to SI before substituting (1 m/s = 3.6 km/h) |
| 2 | Mass vs weight | "The astronaut's mass is lower on the Moon." | Mass (kg) is constant; weight (N) = m·g changes |
| 3 | Heavier objects fall faster | "A 2 kg ball hits the ground before a 1 kg ball." | Without air resistance, all objects fall with the same acceleration |
| 4 | Forgetting friction or other forces | Using applied force instead of net force in F = m·a | Draw a free-body diagram and add all forces |
| 5 | Mixing horizontal and vertical motion | Using horizontal speed to find fall time | Treat the two directions separately |
| 6 | Sign errors | Treating upward velocity and downward g with the same sign | Choose a positive direction and keep it |
| 7 | Parallel resistance larger than a branch | "4 Ω in parallel with 4 Ω gives 8 Ω." | Parallel total is always less than the smallest branch |
| 8 | Speed vs velocity | "Its velocity is 10 m/s." when direction matters | Velocity needs a direction; speed does not |
| 9 | Squaring errors | ½·m·v² computed as (½·m·v)² | Recompute with the formula written out |
| 10 | Unreasonable magnitude | A car accelerating at 300 m/s² | Compare with everyday values (g ≈ 9.8 m/s²) |

## Quick review checklist
- [ ] Units consistent and in the final answer
- [ ] Correct formula for the situation (constant acceleration? energy conserved?)
- [ ] All forces included
- [ ] Sign convention consistent
- [ ] Answer checked with a second method or an order-of-magnitude estimate
